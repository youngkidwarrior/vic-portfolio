## Building the company and the product

Shenanigan began with a founder question: could a community coordinate ownership, contribution, and product development around a shared technology platform?

Victor bootstrapped the company, raised more than $100,000 through community support, recruited six recurring contributors and two contractors, and led work across applications, smart contracts, infrastructure, and governance.

## Extending the runway

PANTS was a community fundraising and token-reward mechanism designed when the business needed a new way forward. It aligned participation with support and extended the company’s operating runway by 12 months.

The mechanism was not an isolated experiment. It emerged from the same product system as the community, governance, and contribution model around it.

## Participation as infrastructure

Victor designed decentralized governance and contribution systems that rewarded stakeholders according to measurable participation. The work combined product mechanics, incentive design, Solidity, frontend experience, and the practical work of operating a small team.

## What carried forward

Shenanigan sharpened the habits that define Victor’s later work: own the whole system, keep the product legible, and make technical architecture answer to real human behavior.
